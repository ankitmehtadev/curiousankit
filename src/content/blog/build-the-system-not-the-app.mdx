I am not a "productivity guru". I am still exploring the psychology, theories and processes behind productivity. Productivity, in my view, is how efficiently and effectively I am able to manage my time and complete my tasks.

<Level n={1} title="The tool trap" />

I first started looking into this subject during its boom in 2020. Since then, I have noticed a sudden jump in the number of productivity apps, blogs, YouTube channels and books, and it is still a very popular topic. After going through a lot of productivity material online, joining various forums, attending numerous webinars and reading countless books, I can now say that I finally understand the gist of it.

I love trying out the latest products on the market. So when I was introduced to this topic, I excitedly jumped onto every tool out there. I signed up for their beta or trial versions and migrated all of my content onto each of them, changing platforms every few days. Eventually, I realised that I was wasting a lot of my time and energy just setting up tools and migrating my data to the next fancy product. Well, isn't this quite the opposite of being productive?

Then I came across a book, *Building a Second Brain* by Tiago Forte. That's where I realised that one shouldn't focus on the tools, but on the system.

<PullQuote>Don't focus on the tools. Focus on the system.</PullQuote>

<Level n={2} title="No system fits everyone" />

There is no "one system fits all" in this world. Some people enjoy writing down all of their tasks for the day, prioritising them and ticking them off once done (the GTD method). Others prefer writing down just the three most important tasks for the day and ticking those off (the Bento method). And while some prefer responding to emails first thing in the morning, I reserve my afternoons and late nights for it.

<Level n={3} title="Five things that shape your system" />

I believe how one's productivity system is built depends on these factors.

### Routine

The routine of a company's CEO will differ from that of a software developer in the same company. The routine of a single person living in a rented apartment will be different from that of a married person with kids living in similar accommodation. Professional and family circumstances greatly affect one's routine. While one person might have the time and freedom to design their productivity system, for someone else, life itself might design it for them.

### Motivation

One person might enjoy tackling their most challenging task first thing in the morning, followed by the easier tasks (the "Eat the frog" method). Another might prefer gradually building their confidence throughout the day by finishing the simpler tasks first (the "Easy wins" method). It boils down to how you can keep yourself motivated throughout the day to achieve your goals.

### Priorities

What are your priorities? Is it spending as much time with family as possible? Becoming the boss of your company as soon as "yesterday"? Partying every night with friends? Or finally finishing that book you have been promising everyone you know?

### Technology

iOS vs Android: the never ending war. I'm not a fanboy of either, but I do use an iPhone, an Apple Watch Ultra and an M2 Pro MacBook Pro. My work laptop is an old, refurbished Dell running Windows. When it comes to my personal productivity, I lean towards products that work with Apple platforms. That doesn't mean tools without Apple compatibility aren't powerful enough. In the past I have tried purely web based and cross platform apps, but somehow I always ended up coming back to the ones I use today.

### Budget

You could be someone who can afford a calendar app costing $300 a year to keep track of your schedule, or someone who is happy with the free version of a to do list app, even if it doesn't tick all the boxes. One thing I have noticed is that privacy, quality, reliability and support always come at a price, and I believe they should.
