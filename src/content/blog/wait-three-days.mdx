I get it. Trust me. It is already hard enough to earn money. It is even more exhausting to save and grow the money you earn. It takes time, discipline, an understanding of yourself, practice and patience. If you are too hard on yourself every single day for not earning or saving enough, you lose faith in yourself and your ability to ever do it. I have been there and done that. But the important thing is… I got out of that negative self talk.

I have been impatient, followed the wrong financial advice and felt too demotivated to change anything for the better. After trying multiple ways of managing my money, I have realised my many mistakes. My whole idea of money and its importance had been wrong all along, and I had to shake it up in my head a little.

Finally, I came across the buckets system and set it up. Then I wondered what to do next. How do I build patience, discipline and clarity around managing my hard earned money?

Here are some tips I follow to manage my day to day finances.

<KeyIdea label="Before you start" variant="outline">These tips are super simple and generic, but I am not a financial adviser or an expert. I am still making mistakes and learning from them every single day. So before you take any crazy financial steps, please talk to a registered and certified financial adviser.</KeyIdea>

<Level n={1} title="Can't wait to buy it? Wait for 3 days." />

*"We are launching the new iPhone with the most powerful processor, the best camera, the brightest and most advanced display, and the craziest features. Pre-order it NOW to get it delivered FIRST."* Apple, every year.

I know you are super excited to click that "Pre-order now" button… but wait. Wait for 3 days.

Ask yourself these questions:

1. Do I need it?
2. Will I be as excited to use it in 2 months as I am right now?
3. This item costs the equivalent of 3 weeks of my salary. Is it worth it?
4. How much of a difference will it make in my life?
5. How much happiness will I get by buying it right now?

If the answers to these questions are satisfactory and make sense to you, go buy it and enjoy! Mental health is super important too! At the end of the day, you must understand the difference between your wants and your needs. No two people will have the same definition of wants and needs in life.

By the way, this applies to every luxury you buy in life (not just the latest iPhone).

<Level n={2} title="Save water for the hot summer" />

If you don't already have one, open an emergency bank account right now! I'm serious, go online and open one right now!

There are a few rules to follow with this account, and trust me, it will take a lot of discipline.

1. This account should be with a different bank from the one you use for your day to day transactions.
2. It should hold enough money for you to survive 4 to 6 months without any income.
3. Remember, this is an emergency account, only to be used on your rainy days. Under no circumstances should the money in it be used for anything else (including pre-ordering that latest iPhone).
4. There is a high chance this money will never be used, or will only be used after a long time. So why not let it grow as much as possible? Open it as a savings account with the bank that pays you the highest interest rate.
5. Once you have 4 to 6 months of savings in this account, keep transferring a tiny percentage of your monthly income to it.

<Level n={3} title="Invest, invest, invest" label="Deeper still" short="Level 3" />

Got your salary? Paid off the bills, enjoyed some fancy dinners and splurged on some new clothes? What about the remaining money? Are you planning to earn some interest on those savings in the bank? How much interest is your bank's savings account paying?

It is a well known fact that the stock market has historically outperformed both inflation and bank savings account interest rates.

I know, it is a confusing and overwhelming area. But you are not alone. There are plenty of resources online that explain every single step of the process, no matter which country you live in.

I personally only invest in ETFs (thank you for the tip, Mr Buffett!). ETFs, or exchange traded funds, offer a lower risk, lower cost and more diversified way to invest in the market.

My personal investment portfolio in Australia is built with 3 low cost ETFs: IVV (50%), VEU (25%) and VAS (25%). I will cover these in more detail in future posts. Between them, these ETFs cover the top 500 companies in the United States, developed and emerging markets outside the US, and the top 300 companies in Australia.

I don't suggest investing all your savings in the stock market (even if it is just ETFs), but I do recommend investing the majority of them, as long as your emergency account has enough money for the rainy days.

Please talk to your financial adviser and accountant before making investment decisions.

<Level n={4} title="Honey, I bought an alpaca… You did what!?" />

Yep, I know you saw this coming. Every financial adviser and personal finance educator starts their advice with "Make a monthly budget and stick to it". So I am doing something different this time… I am saving it for the end of this post.

First of all, planning a monthly household budget is boring and difficult. Sticking to it is even more painful. And tracking your spending is excruciating. I get it. I have been there, and it isn't pretty.

There are a few steps that make this process simple. Again, it will take a lot of discipline.

Start by sitting down with your partner and writing down your goals for the next 3, 5 and 10 years. For example: we will have $150,000 in our investment portfolio by the end of the next 3 years. We will own a new property by the end of 5 years. We will start our own business by the end of the next 10 years.

Now open your bank transactions and note down all your expenses for the previous month. Every single thing. Divide them into categories. I use these:

- Groceries
- Medical (doctors, medicines and medical tests)
- Personal grooming (haircuts, facials, spa treatments, etc.)
- Gifts (for each other, friends, family and yourself)
- Shopping
- Entertainment (movie tickets, sports, shows, etc.)
- Eating out (including takeaway)
- Coffee and tea
- Fuel
- Fixed (bills, insurance premiums, car parking)
- Home improvement (including plants and light globes)
- Investments

This shows you how much you spend on average in each category every month. You might notice that you are spending more than necessary in some categories and want to cut back there to meet your goals. Do that, then calculate the total.

This is your realistic budget. It includes both your lifestyle and your savings goals.

Finally, create a spreadsheet with the dates of the month in the first column, from the first day to the last, and your categories across the top row.

Every single night before going to bed, open your list of bank transactions, check the amount of every transaction, add them up by category and enter the totals in your spreadsheet against today's date. At the end of the month, check the total spent in each category and see how you are tracking against your budget.

Chances are you won't crack it in the first three months. But from the fourth month onwards, you will become a master at creating, tracking and maintaining your budget. Don't be too hard on yourself.

That's it. You can do it. You can take control of your money. I believe in you! The main aim of this exercise is to get comfortable with money and build a healthy relationship with it. Money is not something to be scared of. It should work for you more than you work for it.
