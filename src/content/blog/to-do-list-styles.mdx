Hey there, productivity enthusiasts and list making champions! Are you all too familiar with the excitement of creating a dazzling to do list, diligently ticking off items for the first few days, and then somehow getting lost in a sea of tasks, feeling overwhelmed or losing track of your goals? Have you ever been tempted to blame your to do list app and jump ship to a different one, hoping it would better support you in conquering your daily goals? If you've experienced that all too relatable scenario, rest assured, you're not alone. Welcome aboard my "been there, done that" boat! Chances are that your work style is not aligned with your to do list management style. But fear not, because we're going to navigate the choppy waters of to do list management and discover strategies that will keep you sailing smoothly towards your tasks and goals.

I have discovered four styles of to do list management. There are many more, but only these four made sense to me after I tried each one. They are simple to understand and follow, although not every style will work for everyone. If you've tried a particular style and found it hard to stick with, it may not be the right fit for you.

I believe 5 factors can help you identify the perfect to do list management style:

- **Relationship with time:** How accurately can you estimate the time each task on your daily list will take? Do most of your tasks have specific time constraints?
- **Visualisation preference:** Do you prefer to see all your tasks in a single view so you can plan your day better?
- **Handling multiple jobs or projects:** Do you prefer keeping multiple lists, each dedicated to a specific job or project?
- **Procrastination and distractions:** Do you tend to leave tasks until the last moment, or get easily distracted when dealing with several tasks at once?
- **Organisation skills:** How much time are you willing to invest in organising your to do list(s)?

By considering these factors, you can find a to do list management style that suits your unique preferences and boosts your productivity. Remember, experimentation is key, and it's perfectly normal to try out different styles before finding the one that works best for you.

So, without further ado, here are the 4 major styles of to do list management.

<Level n={1} title="Task Manager" />

This is the simplest style of to do list. You create a running list of tasks and check them off as you complete them. You can use traditional pen and paper, or make use of the power of technology.

There are plenty of apps out there that make this a better experience with features like these:

- **Add a deadline:** set specific deadlines for each task to keep yourself accountable and prioritise effectively.
- **Upload files or add detailed notes:** attach files or add notes to tasks, so all the necessary information is readily available.
- **Set recurring tasks:** automate repetitive tasks by setting them as recurring, saving you the time and effort of entering them again.
- **Assign tasks to other people:** delegate tasks to others, which makes it an excellent tool for collaborative projects and teamwork.
- **Set reminder notifications:** get timely reminders so you stay on top of your tasks and meet your deadlines.

However, when using the Task Manager style, it's crucial to be precise with your task descriptions to avoid confusion, especially with similar tasks. With this style, it is also very easy to get lost in the ever growing list. On the plus side, it lets you automate your task list (with recurring tasks) and reminds you of deadlines.

Some recommended apps: Things 3 (which I personally use), Todoist and TickTick.

<Level n={2} title="No List, Just a Calendar" />

When managing a long list of tasks, it's natural to feel overwhelmed, particularly if you juggle multiple jobs and side projects like I do. In that case, the Task Manager style might not be the best fit. Instead, consider a time blocking approach and add all your tasks directly to your calendar.

This method has some benefits:

- **Comprehensive view:** with all your tasks in your calendar, you can easily see your schedule and commitments across different parts of your life.
- **Dedicated time:** assigning a specific time block to each task encourages focused work and reduces the chances of procrastination.
- **Priority management:** you can prioritise tasks effectively and give them suitable time slots based on their importance and urgency.

However, this method does come with some downsides. It only works if you know how much time each task will take, and some people get overwhelmed looking at a completely full calendar.

To manage your tasks in this style, you can use any calendar app, such as Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook Calendar or Notion Calendar (formerly Cron).

<Level n={3} title="Bento Method" label="Deeper still" short="Level 3" />

This one is my personal favourite.

We all have numerous tasks in a day. Sometimes it is easy to track them in a running list (the Task Manager method), but sometimes it is easy to lose focus and work on non urgent tasks. That could be the temptation to procrastinate on challenging tasks, or simply being overwhelmed by our day to day responsibilities.

That's when the Bento Method comes to the rescue. Its brilliance lies in its simplicity: it prompts you to review all your tasks and carefully select the three most important ones. These are:

1. **Task 1:** the most crucial and time sensitive task.
2. **Task 2:** an equally important and time sensitive task.
3. **Task 3:** a smaller, yet significant task.

You spend the most time on Task 1, followed by Task 2 and then Task 3. The main goal is to complete these three tasks by the end of the day, even if it means leaving other items on your regular task list untouched.

This method is extremely powerful if you follow the "Eat the frog" productivity system, which encourages tackling your toughest and most critical task first. The beauty of this method is its ability to sharpen your focus, boost your confidence and give you the motivation you need to tackle the rest of your daily tasks.

<Level n={4} title="The 1 Task" />

This style is similar to the Bento Method, but stricter. It goes like this:

1. Begin by listing all the tasks you wish to accomplish in the day. This list is your foundation.
2. Analyse each task's importance and urgency, and rank them in order of significance.
3. Choose the task in the top spot on your priority list. This single task becomes your sole focus for the day.
4. Dedicate a separate space, perhaps a sticky note or a section on your digital device, to write down this chosen task. This written reminder is your constant guide throughout the day.
5. Dive into that top priority task and work on it diligently until it is done. The satisfaction of finishing it will make your day truly successful.

This method improves your focus and helps build your prioritisation skills. It doesn't allow multitasking, so you can't switch between tasks. And if the one task you choose is difficult, there is a high chance you will procrastinate.

So I recommend this method for people with high levels of self motivation and an unwavering ability to stay focused. It is a powerful tool for refining your prioritisation skills and accomplishing your most essential goals.

The journey towards productivity is a dynamic process, full of learning and growth. With the right to do list management style, we can confidently steer our ship towards accomplishment, balance and success in both our personal and professional lives. So, productivity enthusiasts and list making champions, embark on this exciting voyage and let the power of an optimised to do list propel you towards achievement and fulfilment!
