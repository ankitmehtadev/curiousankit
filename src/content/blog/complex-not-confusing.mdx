In my career as an IT professional, I have noticed that many people and companies struggle with designing interfaces, cracking the user experience and building mockups. There is often a dilemma between making something complex and making something beautiful. Yes, I said "beautiful" and not "simple". Something complex in the back end doesn't have to be confusing in the front end.

I have come across many apps that are packed with amazing features and very well thought out back end code, but simply lack a good interface. The outcome? Either I feel the system is too complicated to even consider using, or, if I do use it, I lose interest in no time. Yes, I said I "feel" and not "think".

<PullQuote>An interface should be a way to feel a system, not merely a way to work with it.</PullQuote>

I believe an interface should be the key to "feeling" the system rather than merely a way to work with it. Over the years, tech giants have spent billions of dollars hiring psychologists, scientists and techies to build the best user experience (UX) for their customers.

My strong suggestion is to make the most of their existing work in your projects, and make your products, no matter how complex they are, feel awesome!

I have gathered a few resources here that will help you get to that awesome user experience. And if your potential users and friends already think your product looks and "feels" awesome, maybe this will help you make it even better.

<Level n={1} title="Use Apple's design resources" />

If you are designing an iOS app, make use of [Apple's design resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/). They include Apple's official fonts (SF Pro, SF Compact, SF Mono and New York), and all the symbols and UI elements for iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS and tvOS apps. They are all free to download. Use them rather than creating your own symbols from scratch. This will save you time, and your app will look as beautiful as Apple's own apps.

<Level n={2} title="Learn a proper design tool" />

If you are serious about user interface (UI) design and would like to build a career in it, I highly recommend buying [Sketch](https://www.sketch.com/). Unfortunately, it is only available on macOS, but you can use it to design Android apps, web and desktop apps, and of course apps for Apple platforms. It is used by big names for their product and brand design.

If you are looking for an alternative to Sketch, I recommend [Lunacy](https://icons8.com/lunacy). It is free and available on all platforms (including Linux).

<Level n={3} title="Write a design specification" />

If you have received feedback that your UI needs to be standardised, I suggest creating a design specification, or design guidelines, for your product. Most big tech companies have one. That's how, even when thousands of developers from across the world work on a single product, it looks as if it was made by one person. The design specification sets the rules the entire team follows when developing anything and everything related to the product.

Here are some examples of design guidelines:

- [Mozilla Dot Design](https://mozilla.design/firefox/)
- [Microsoft Fluent Design](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/)
- [Google Design](https://design.google/about)
- [Material Design](https://m3.material.io/)
