Did you know that colours play a vital role in the way living beings perceive information? Colours are not just a topic in fine arts. They are a science in themselves. Don't worry, I am not going to get into the physics of a prism turning white light into a rainbow. But if you are interested, read about the [dispersion of light by prisms](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/refrn/Lesson-4/Dispersion-of-Light-by-Prisms) in your spare time. It is really interesting.

<Level n={1} title="A small change, a different feeling" />

Anyway, back to the topic. Colours are so important in delivering information that just changing the hue or saturation value can evoke a completely different feeling. If you are wondering how a petty thing like colour can make such a big difference, ask yourself this: why does the colour white represent peace, or surrender?

<PullQuote>The colours you choose raise different emotions and feelings in your audience.</PullQuote>

<Level n={2} title="Same red, different meanings" />

A colour in one culture can mean something completely different in another. For example, solid red (hex #FF0000, RGB 255, 0, 0) generally represents fire, violence, war, love and passion. But the same red also represents prosperity and happiness in China. In some countries, brides wear red on their wedding day, while in South Africa, red represents mourning. In "western" countries, red represents luxury and elegance. Think about that the next time you walk the "red" carpet!

<Level n={3} title="Why it matters for your interface" />

I know, I know, you might be wondering why this guy is so obsessed with colours. It is because the colours you choose in your user interface designs raise different emotions and feelings in your audience. In fact, and I apologise once again for bringing up red, some studies have found that red [raises blood pressure](https://csef.usc.edu/History/2003/Projects/S0317.pdf) and respiration rate, and even increases metabolism.

One tool I constantly use to choose colours and shades for all my projects is [Coolors](https://coolors.co/generate).

It is a free and intuitive tool for generating colour combinations. You can lock some colours and move them around to see how they match the other colours in the palette. Many contributors have already created some cool colour combinations with it, so you can simply use theirs if you'd like to save time.
